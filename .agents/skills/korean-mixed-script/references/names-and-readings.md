Names and foreign readings
==========================

Evidence and choice of name
---------------------------

Check the person's own public usage or an authoritative institutional source
for the Han spelling and current name. Consult reliable Korean-language usage
to assess recognition. A user-supplied verified spelling can supply the needed
context; do not repeat questions already answered.

For Chinese, Japanese, and Vietnamese loans, identify the original language
and actual orthography. Do not back-form characters from a romanization or
Hangul pronunciation. For Vietnamese names, Han-Nom sources may be needed.
If competing chữ Nôm spellings exist, prefer an overwhelmingly established
Korean-language form. Otherwise summarize candidates, sources, and uncertainty
and ask the user to choose.

For difficult proper names, use independent subagent research when available
and permitted. Give researchers distinct source scopes and require direct
evidence. Repeated copies of the same unsourced spelling are not independent
confirmation. Stop short of a final choice when the evidence remains unclear;
report the research and ask. If delegation is unavailable, disclose that limit
and investigate with the tools actually available.

An actual Han-character personal name can take precedence over a better-known
Western name, with the familiar name in parentheses. Hong Minhee's specified
case is 黃仁勳(Jensen Huang). Annotate the Chinese-name pronunciation as needed
by the output system; Jensen Huang is a familiar-name gloss, not its reading.

A Western scholar's adopted East Asian name does not automatically displace
the name used among Korean readers. Conversely, 朴露子 is the author's specified
name for 박노자 regardless of whether citizenship is the reason for its use.
Origin, nationality, self-identification, and sustained usage matter together;
neither Korean popularity nor ancestry alone is an automatic selection rule.

For Korean names, do not guess characters from sound or infer a surname from
its frequency. Chinese Wikipedia may supply a lead only when its cited source
actually substantiates the spelling. Read that source. An unknown name stays
in Hangul pending evidence. Do not treat a verified native Korean given name
as an invitation to translate it semantically into Hanja.

Use current names. 唐鳳 is the author's specified Chinese name for Audrey Tang;
do not append a former name as background or add deadnames to a glossary.
Institutional renamings are a separate historical-reference question, not an
extension of a person's deadname rule. Retain source identifiers when needed
to make a reference traceable without introducing a deadname.


Regional form rules
-------------------

Normalize Chinese names under the same Kangxi baseline and explicit personal
preferences as Korean lexical text. Routine use of simplified characters in
a Chinese source does not by itself override this rule. Resolve one-to-many
simplified characters by the actual name, not by a single-character converter.

For modern Japanese people born before Japan's defeat in World War II, use
old forms unless the person explicitly established a particular spelling.
For people born after the defeat, use the person's approved spelling, including
new forms, old forms, and name-specific variants. A modern person's explicit
statement about their own character form takes precedence over the birth-period
rule. Preserve distinctions such as 高 U+9AD8 and 髙 U+9AD9.

The exact date dividing the birth periods has not been chosen. Ask when a
borderline birth date makes a difference. Do not invent a legal registration
spelling, infer a variant from a font, or apply a person's birth-period rule
to a company's founding date.

Apply the relevant character-form and evidence rules to other proper names
after choosing the name according to its category below. Use 越南 for Vietnam,
with 베트남 supplied as its intended reading where required. Do not invent
Chinese translations for Western brands or translate a product identifier.


Latin-script people, companies, and products
--------------------------------------------

For a person from a Latin-script language community, keep the name's actual
Latin spelling, including diacritics, case, and name punctuation. This default
applies after the choice between the person's actual names: it does not undo
the specified preference for 黃仁勳(Jensen Huang) or 朴露子. Nor does it turn
a Chinese transliteration assigned to a Western person into that person's
own Han name.

Determine the source language and the Korean loanword spelling of the name
before attaching particles. French names follow French spelling rules,
Portuguese names Portuguese rules, and so on. Use established Korean forms
where the loanword rules recognize them. For example, French Albert Camus
has the Korean form 알베르 카뮈, so write Albert Camus는 and Albert Camus가.
Do not select particles from the final Latin letter, pronounce every name as
English, or replace the Latin name with Hangul merely to attach a particle.
If the language or reading is uncertain, research it; ask if evidence cannot
settle a consequential choice.

Apply this Latin-script default to names of companies in the private sector
and their products, including names outside English-speaking regions.
This includes publicly traded companies; it is not a stock-ownership test.
For a company or product operating in the Korean market, inspect its official
Korean/Korea website.
If the brand uses and prefers an official Hangul name, use that form. If it
retains Latin branding in Korean prose, retain that spelling and capitalization.
Choose particles by the intended Korean reading in either case.

Check running Korean text, product headings, and brand guidance rather than
relying on a logo, a reseller's spelling, or a translated legal footer alone.
For example, the [Microsoft Windows Korean site] uses Microsoft and Windows
in its Korean text, and the [Apple Korea site] uses Apple and iPhone. These
illustrate the rule; check the actual official usage for the current task
rather than assuming all companies have the same preference. Distinguish
the brand from a local subsidiary's legal name when they differ.

[Microsoft Windows Korean site]: https://www.microsoft.com/ko-kr/windows
[Apple Korea site]: https://www.apple.com/kr/


Technologies, projects, and derived organization names
------------------------------------------------------

Apply the company/product brand policy to names that identify a technology,
software project, library, framework, or toolchain. The author's examples
retain Python, Rust, React, Git, and Linux in Latin script. For other names,
apply the official Korean brand policy. Community usage of a generic term
alone does not override a project's official name. Do not treat every
English-looking word in a project's documentation as a proper name. Preserve
actual code identifiers separately: Git names the project, while a literal
`git` command is code. Ordinary terminology such as wheel in Python packaging
follows [the foreign-term rules](foreign-terms.md).

An organization named after a technology or project may retain that selected
proper-name component while the rest follows established Korean wording.
The author's specified forms are Linux 財團 for Linux Foundation and GNU
프로젝트 for GNU Project. This does not preserve an entire English name merely
because one component is a project name.

Use 自由 소프트웨어 財團 for Free Software Foundation. Here free software
corresponds to the established Korean expression 자유 소프트웨어, as in
자유 소프트웨어 운동, rather than 프리 소프트웨어. Apply Hanja to its
Sino-Korean parts: 自由 소프트웨어 運動. Capitalization or an organization's
use of a phrase as its name does not by itself make every word a Latin brand.
Do not replace 자유 with 무료; the concepts differ.

These examples settle those names, not every organization with a technical
connection. Research established Korean wording and the status of each name
component. Ask the user when the evidence cannot settle whether a component
is a retained project/brand name or a phrase to translate or transcribe.


Countries, places, and other organizations
------------------------------------------

The Latin-script default above does not apply wholesale to country names,
place names, or organizations other than companies in the private sector.
The project-name components described above are a specific exception. A
university remains in this category even if it is privately operated; political
parties and international organizations also belong here.

Use an established Korean name, including a conventional Han name, before
constructing a new transcription or translation. Otherwise distinguish the
elements by their meaning to contemporary speakers of the source language:
transcribe an opaque proper-name element into Hangul under Korean loanword
spelling rules, and translate recognizable words or morphemes. Write any
Sino-Korean parts of the resulting name in Hanja. Do not translate an obscure
historical etymology, mechanically split a name into roots, or replace a
conventional name with a newly coined literal translation.

The author's specified choices are:

| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Canada                                                | 캐나다                          |
| Deutschland                                           | 獨逸                            |
| Советский Союз                                        | 소비에트 聯邦; abbreviated 蘇聯 |
| Roma, the Italian capital                             | 로마                            |
| European Union / Europäische Union / Union européenne | 유럽 聯合                       |
| Partido Comunista Português                           | 포르투갈 共產黨                 |
| Việt Cộng                                             | 越共                            |
| Harvard University                                    | 하버드 大學校                   |

In ordinary prose, use 유럽 聯合 rather than EU. Use 蘇聯, never 소聯, for
the Soviet Union's abbreviation: an established Korean Han abbreviation takes
precedence over converting only the surviving syllables of a foreign phrase.
Likewise, use the established 越共 rather than retaining Việt Cộng in Latin
script. The country's standard Hangul spelling is 포르투갈, not
포루투갈. Introduce unabbreviated names where practicable under the general
first-mention rule, without replacing the specified forms with Latin initials.

These are name-selection rules, not permission to change a quoted speaker's
vocabulary. Preserve Korean quotations under the quotation rules, and retain
original spelling where the original name itself is being discussed or in a
literal citation, URL, or identifier.


Readings
--------

Keep the Han spelling, source language, intended Hangul spelling, and familiar
alternative name distinct. Use Korean loanword spelling rules for local-language
readings. Check established Korean forms and primary pronunciation evidence
rather than mechanically pronouncing each character with Korean Hanja readings.

This does not require replacing established Korean names such as 中國 and
日本 with Mandarin or Japanese transcriptions. Distinguish an established
Korean lexical name from a local-language loan; check the applicable convention
or ask when the choice is unclear.

In HTML-capable Markdown or HTML, use language/script tags on the base and
reading, with fallback parentheses:

~~~~ html
<ruby lang="vi-Hani">胡志明<rp>(</rp><rt lang="vi-Hang">호찌민</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>
~~~~

Use BCP 47 to distinguish language from script. Hanja in Korean prose is not
automatically Chinese. The example deliberately represents Vietnamese using
Han characters and a Hangul transcription. Do not replace its language tags
with Hangulize's three-letter model codes. Choose appropriate language/script
tags for other entries; do not invent unregistered subtags.

When HTML/ruby is unavailable, use 胡志明(호찌민). With an actual configured
converter, its verified reading mapping can provide the annotation. A general
claim that the converter exists is insufficient evidence that a particular
entry, occurrence, or output channel has the right reading.
